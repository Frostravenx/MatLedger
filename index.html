Load your Google Maps Timeline JSON files

Open your phone's Settings app → Location → Location Services → Timeline → Export Timeline data → Continue → Save.

The file saves to your Downloads folder. It may be named Timeline.json or location-history.json depending on your device.

Google Takeout no longer works. In late 2024, Google moved Timeline data from the cloud to on-device storage. Takeout exports now return only encrypted backups — no usable location data. Export directly from your phone instead.

Have an old Takeout export? If you downloaded a JSON file from Google Takeout before the 2024 migration, it will still load and parse correctly here.

Easiest — use this app directly on your phone. Open this app in your phone's browser, tap the Data tab, and load your Timeline.json straight from your Downloads folder. No transfer needed.

Prefer to use a desktop? Location history files are often 50–100MB+, so email won't work. Best options:

Google Drive / Dropbox — upload on phone, download on computer.
USB cable — plug in, copy the file directly.
AirDrop — instant if you're on Android with a Mac nearby (via Nearby Share) or already on iPhone.

All processing happens entirely in your browser — your location data is never uploaded anywhere.
⚠️ Heads up — most iPhone users won't have useful data here. Google Maps Timeline only tracks you if you've had the Google Maps app installed with Always location access enabled for an extended period — ideally years. If that's not you, your export will likely be empty or very sparse.

If you do have long-running Google Maps location history: open Google Maps → tap your profile picture → Your Timeline → tap the ⋯ menu (top-right) → Location and privacy settings → Export Timeline data → Save to Files.

Google Takeout doesn't work for iPhone either. Since 2024, Timeline is stored on-device for everyone — Android and iPhone alike. Takeout only returns encrypted backup files now, not usable JSON.

Session Filter Settings

Mat Ledger decides whether a detected location visit counts as a training session based on how long it lasted. Visits shorter than the minimum are likely GPS noise or quick stops — not real training. Visits longer than the maximum are likely the app misreading an all-day location stay. Adjust these if sessions you know happened are missing, or if false matches are appearing. Changes apply immediately and reprocess all loaded data.
